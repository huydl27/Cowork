# Genre Landscape: Casual / Hyper-Casual Mobile Games
*Market Research Report — March 2026*

---

## 1. Market Overview

The global mobile gaming market generated approximately **$126 billion** in total revenue in 2025, with in-app purchases (IAP) alone accounting for $81.75 billion. Mobile gaming now represents more than half of all gaming industry earnings, supported by an active player base of **3 billion users** worldwide.

Within this ecosystem, the casual and hyper-casual segment commands **the highest download volume of any genre**: hyper-casual titles recorded **22 billion downloads** in 2025, with casual games adding another 14.3 billion. In the first half of 2025 alone, the top 100 hyper-casual titles generated **2.4 billion downloads** and **$345 million in IAP revenue** — implying a ~$690M full-year IAP run rate for the segment's top performers.

---

## 2. Genre Evolution: From Hyper-Casual to Hybrid-Casual

The hyper-casual category has undergone a structural transformation. Pure hyper-casual — defined by single-mechanic gameplay, zero onboarding friction, and nearly 100% ad-based monetization — is no longer competitively viable as a standalone strategy. Rising user acquisition (UA) costs, thin ad ARPU, and weak long-term retention have forced studios to evolve.

**Hybrid-casual** has emerged as the dominant new format: studios layer meta-game systems (progression trees, collection mechanics, building loops, light narrative) onto a simple, intuitive core mechanic. The result is a game that retains the low-friction entry of hyper-casual while generating the engagement depth and higher LTV of casual mid-core titles.

Key evidence of this shift:

- Hybrid-casual IAP revenue grew from **$390M → $733M** in 2025 (+88% YoY)
- Hybrid-casual year-over-year growth rate: **37%** (fastest-growing mobile segment)
- Pure hyper-casual ad ARPU is declining; hybrid models combining ads + IAP + subscriptions have increased total per-user revenue by **14% YoY**

## 5. User Acquisition & Retention Benchmarks

### Cost Per Install (CPI)
- Hyper-casual average CPI: **$1.50 (Android)** / **$2.50 (iOS)**
- Best-in-class (viral / TikTok-driven): as low as **$0.20**
- Broad mobile gaming CPI (2025): $0.56 average, **up 30% YoY** (Adjust 2026 Report)

### Retention Benchmarks
| Day | Industry Average | Top Performer Target |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | 27% | ≥ 40% |
| Day 7 | 13% | ≥ 20% |
| Day 14 | 8% | ≥ 12% |
| Day 30 | 5% | ≥ 8% |

Day-1 retention ≥ 40% is the primary green-light signal for scale. Games below 30% D1 are unlikely to sustain profitable UA spend.

### LTV & Profitability
- Estimated average LTV for pure hyper-casual: **~$2.80**
- Hybrid-casual LTV meaningfully higher due to combined IAP + ad revenue streams
- Profitability requires LTV > CPI with sufficient margin — achievable with hybrid model + organic/viral UA contribution

---

## 6. Key Strategic Takeaways

1. **Pure hyper-casual is a losing bet in isolation.** Rising CPIs, low ARPU, and D30 retention of ~5% make scaled profitability nearly impossible for new entrants competing head-to-head with AZUR, Voodoo, or SayGames.

2. **Hybrid-casual is the opportunity.** The fastest-growing segment, with IAP revenue nearly doubling YoY and strong upside for studios that can build a compelling meta layer on top of a proven mechanic.

3. **Puzzle and simulation mechanics remain the most defensible core loops** — high install velocity, global accessibility, strong retention-per-session, and natural upsell vectors (no-ads, extra lives, cosmetics).

4. **Viral-first UA is the cost-efficiency lever.** TikTok and YouTube Shorts gameplay clips are generating CPIs 5–10× cheaper than paid UA channels. Games with inherently shareable mechanics have a structural advantage.

5. **LiveOps is the revenue multiplier.** Time-limited events, seasonal content, and battle passes are now standard practice. Studios without a LiveOps roadmap leave significant revenue on the table.
