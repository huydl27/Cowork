## The Opportunity in One Sentence

The hybrid-casual mobile segment is the fastest-growing category in gaming (+88% IAP revenue YoY), and no breakout title yet owns the physics-based block-stacking puzzle niche — **STACKRIFT** is designed to be that title.

## Part 1: Why Now — The Market Case

### The Mobile Gaming Market Is Enormous and Still Growing

Global mobile gaming generated **$126 billion** in revenue in 2025. There are **3 billion** active mobile gamers. Mobile now accounts for more than half of all gaming industry earnings. This is not a niche bet — it is the mainstream.

Within this market, casual and hyper-casual games drive more downloads than any other genre: **22 billion hyper-casual downloads** and 14.3 billion casual downloads in 2025 alone. This is the broadest possible audience base for a game to enter.

### The Genre Is Shifting — And That Creates a Gap

The pure hyper-casual format that dominated 2019–2022 is in structural decline. Rising user acquisition costs ($1.50–$2.50 CPI vs. ~$0.20 three years ago), weak retention (D30 at 5% industry average), and eroding ad ARPU have made it nearly impossible for new entrants to build a profitable pure hyper-casual title.

The market has responded with **hybrid-casual** — games that combine the low-friction one-tap entry of hyper-casual with meta-game depth (progression trees, seasonal content, battle passes, leaderboards) borrowed from mid-core titles. The result is longer sessions, higher LTV, and more durable retention. Hybrid-casual IAP revenue grew from $390M to **$733M in a single year**. It is now the #1 fastest-growing segment in mobile gaming.

The opportunity: the hybrid-casual space is growing rapidly, but the puzzle-physics sub-niche within it remains uncrowded. The top publishers (Voodoo, AZUR, Homa, SayGames) are active in the space but have not yet produced a defining physics-launch puzzle title with deep meta-layer integration. That is the gap STACKRIFT is built to fill.

## Part 2: The Game — STACKRIFT

### What It Is

STACKRIFT is a one-thumb hybrid-casual puzzle game. Players stack, balance, and launch colored blocks across crumbling platforms to reach an exit portal — before the rift swallows everything. It is designed to be understood in under 10 seconds and replayed for months.

### The Core Loop (3 Actions)

1. **Tap to catch** — falling blocks land on the player's stack
2. **Swipe to launch** — the stack fires across a gap, building a bridge or triggering switches
3. **Jump to escape** — the player crosses the bridge before the platform crumbles

That's it. The entire experience emerges from three gestures. Difficulty scales through color combo mechanics (matching colors produce effects: freeze, bounce, explode), platform types (crumbling, moving, electrified), and the ever-accelerating rift zone that chases the player across every level.

**Session length:** 45–90 seconds per level. Portrait mode, one hand, no attention required.

### Why This Mechanic Has Breakout Potential

The mechanic hits the three proven triggers of hyper-casual retention:

First, it is **immediately comprehensible** — no tutorial text, no loading screens. The first block falls and the player's instinct to catch it teaches the mechanic.

Second, it has a **satisfying feedback loop** — block stacking is tactile, the launch arc is visually satisfying, and the escape jump produces a moment of genuine tension followed by relief or failure.

Third, it reliably triggers the **"one more try" reflex** — near-miss failures (the stack toppled at the last second, one wrong color ruined the combo) produce an immediate desire to retry. This is the fundamental loop that drives session frequency and organic word-of-mouth.

The mechanic also generates natural **TikTok clip moments**: spectacular combo chains, impossible saves, epic collapses. These shareable moments are a built-in UA channel. Games like Helix Jump, Sand Balls, and Flappy Bird all had this property — STACKRIFT is designed for it from day one.

### The Meta Layer — What Makes It Hybrid

Beyond the core loop, STACKRIFT has three retention-driving meta systems that distinguish it from pure hyper-casual and drive long-term engagement:

**The Rift Codex (Progression Tree):** Players earn Rift Shards from completed levels and combo bonuses. Shards unlock permanent upgrades — platform tools, enhanced block effects, character cosmetics. The progression tree provides a 60–90 day arc before a season reset, giving players a continuous reason to return.

**World Zones (Episodic Content):** The game launches with 3 fully built zones (180 levels), each with a distinct visual biome and new mechanics introduced progressively: Ancient Ruins, Neon City, Ice Caverns. Three additional zones release post-launch via LiveOps drops every 6–8 weeks. This creates a content calendar that extends engagement and provides reactivation triggers.

**Daily Rift Run (Competitive Mode):** Every day, all players receive the same procedurally-generated level seed. A global leaderboard tracks completion scores. Top-100 players earn exclusive cosmetic rewards. This system drives D7 and D30 retention through social competition without requiring expensive guild or multiplayer infrastructure.

### Visual & Audio Identity

The art direction targets a bold, saturated flat-3D aesthetic — geometric clarity with expressive color palettes that shift across biomes. The reference points are *Monument Valley* (spatial clarity) and *Fall Guys* (expressive characters, high-contrast color). The visual style is designed to be eye-catching in a 3-second TikTok scroll and accessible on mid-range devices at 60fps.

Audio design prioritizes tactile satisfaction: each block catch, each launch, each level complete is a distinct, rewarding sound. Adaptive background music escalates with rift proximity, creating genuine tension without becoming fatiguing over long sessions.

### Team & Scope

The project requires a lean team of **8 FTE**: 2 game designers, 2 engineers, 1 artist, 1 animator, 1 UA/growth specialist, 1 producer/PM. The build timeline runs from Month 1 (pre-production) through Month 8 (global launch), with a soft launch at Month 5 providing a data gate before full UA spend is deployed.

## Part 3: The Business Model

### Hybrid Monetization — No Single Point of Failure

STACKRIFT uses a hybrid monetization model combining four revenue streams. This reflects the industry-proven reality that no single stream is sufficient: ad-only LTV (~$2.80) does not cover current CPIs; IAP-only locks out the majority of players who will never spend.

**Rewarded video ads** form the foundation of free-user revenue. Players opt in to watch short ads in exchange for meaningful rewards: a second chance on a failed level, doubled shard earnings, a bonus power-up. Target: 3–5 impressions per DAU at $20–$35 eCPM (US, iOS). This stream is opt-in and never blocks progress.

**Interstitial ads** show at natural session break points only — Zone transitions and every 5 level completions — capped at 4 per session. Overuse of interstitials is the #1 driver of uninstalls in the genre; our cap is conservative by design.

**In-app purchases** cover three product types. A Starter Pack ($2.99) offered at the 72-hour mark targets the first-purchase moment with high-value bundled contents. Shard Bundles ($0.99–$19.99) allow players to accelerate Rift Codex progression. A direct Cosmetic Shop sells character skins, trail effects, and themed UI packs ($0.99–$4.99), updated each Season.

**The Rift Pass**, launching at Month 10, is a 6-week battle pass at $4.99/season. It includes 2× shard rewards, an exclusive seasonal cosmetic set, and a bonus Zone chapter. Battle pass holders in hybrid-casual titles show 35–45% higher D30 retention than non-payers — it is simultaneously a revenue driver and a retention lever.

### Unit Economics

The blended LTV target is **$6.20** across all user segments. At a target CPI of $1.50–$2.50, this yields an **LTV:CPI ratio of 2.5–4.1×** — above the 2× minimum required for sustainable scaled UA spend. The breakdown by user segment:

65% of players are ad-only users who churn by D30, contributing ~$1.20 LTV each. 20% are engaged free users retained past D30 on rewarded ad revenue ($4.80 LTV). 10% are light spenders making 1–2 IAP purchases ($12.00 LTV). 4% are mid-spenders engaging with the Rift Pass and bundle products ($38.00 LTV). 1% are high-value repeat purchasers ($120+ LTV). The model is not dependent on whales — the mid-tier cohort drives the majority of IAP revenue.

## Part 4: Go-to-Market Strategy

### The Framework: Viral-First, Data-Gated

We do not spend on scaled UA until retention is proven. This is the single most important principle of the GTM plan. Too many mobile game budgets have been destroyed by scaling a title before soft launch data confirmed the core loop works. Our approach is staged and evidence-driven.

### Pre-Launch (Months 1–4): Build Organic Infrastructure

Before spending a dollar on paid UA, we build the channels that will reduce our long-term CPI. This means: creating and seeding @playSTACKRIFT social accounts (TikTok, Instagram) with 2× organic gameplay clips per week starting Month 3; conducting App Store Optimization (ASO) keyword research and A/B testing icons and screenshots in TestFlight and Play Console; localizing the store listing into English, German, Portuguese (BR), Indonesian, and Thai; and targeting 5,000+ organic followers and a top-30 ASO keyword ranking before soft launch day one.

The creative content strategy is built around the game's inherent clip potential — we produce 15-second portrait-mode gameplay videos designed for TikTok's algorithm, using trending audio and no voiceover. These are free or near-free to produce and have demonstrated CPI equivalency of $0.20–$0.50 when used as paid creative later.

### Soft Launch (Months 5–7): Validate Before Scaling

Soft launch runs across four markets chosen for data quality and cost efficiency: Australia, Canada, Philippines, Sweden. Total budget: **$50,000**, split 60/30/10 across Meta, Google UAC, and TikTok Ads. Target: 50,000–80,000 installs.

After 30 days, we apply a strict green/yellow/red framework across five metrics: D1 retention, D7 retention, average session length, D30 IAP conversion, and projected LTV:CPI ratio. Any "red" metric triggers an iteration cycle before further spend. Full green across all five metrics immediately triggers global launch preparation.

### Global Launch (Month 8): Activate Paid UA at Scale

Global launch targets Tier 1 markets first (US, UK, Germany, Australia, Canada), expanding to Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets based on CPI and ROAS performance. Monthly UA budget: **$80,000–$120,000**, allocated 40% Meta, 25% Google UAC, 25% TikTok Ads, 10% Apple Search Ads.

Creative strategy calls for 2-week refresh cycles across three tested archetypes: the fail-first hook (shows epic fail then the perfect run), the challenge hook ("can you beat this combo?"), and the satisfying loop (ASMR-style perfect run). Top-performing organic content is repurposed as paid creative, further reducing production costs. An App Store feature request is submitted to Apple's editorial team 6 weeks pre-launch.

### Post-Launch LiveOps (Months 9–18): Retain and Monetize

A structured LiveOps calendar drives retention and reactivation through the first 18 months: a Halloween Rift event at Month 9, Season 1 Rift Pass launch at Month 10, a Winter Zone event at Month 11, Zone 4 content drop at Month 12, and Anniversary + Zone 5–6 at Month 14. Each event doubles as a reactivation trigger for lapsed users via push notification and email campaigns.

At Month 18, targets are: **1.2M DAU**, **4M MAU**, **$250,000/month revenue**, **4.5+ App Store rating**.

Hybrid-casual is the fastest-growing mobile gaming segment. Puzzle mechanics drive the highest install velocity in the genre. The physics-launch puzzle niche is undercrowded. The market data is unusually clear: this is the right genre, the right format, and the right moment.
